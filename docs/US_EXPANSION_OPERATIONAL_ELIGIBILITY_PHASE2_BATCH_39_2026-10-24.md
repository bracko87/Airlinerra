Phase 1 already covers all 1,929 candidates. Phase 2 is a source-based operational assessment, **not gameplay approval or a legal clearance for airline flights**. A hold is not necessarily an airport-wide permanent closure, and absent scheduled-airline evidence is not proof that charter or cargo operations cannot exist. Judge each aircraft against current runway-specific usable landing distance, surface and gear/weight load, weather/terrain, active FAA notices, fuel and ground services, airport-owner permissions and actual Part121/135/139 requirements.

## Critical individual findings

**VMR — Harold Davidson Field, South Dakota.** [Vermillion city regulations](https://www.vermillion.us/149/Airport) require every commercial operator to obtain a **city permit** and meet municipal Minimum Standards before commercial activity. A pilot or FAA aircraft operating certificate alone does not satisfy the separate owner requirement.

**ZEF — Elkin Municipal, North Carolina.** [FAA airport remarks](https://www.airnav.com/airport/ZEF) identify significant **runway-end and adjacent drop-offs, RW25 uphill gradient, unmarked powerlines, trees, deer and PAPI viewing restrictions**. No generic runway-length-only aircraft admission is safe; obtain runway-specific performance and approach/obstacle analysis.

**X26 — Sebastian Municipal, Florida.** [City pilots' page](https://www.cityofsebastian.org/384/Pilots-Information) lists recently resurfaced paved RW05/23 as **4,024 × 150ft**, whereas existing FAA-derived source/registry entries list a **75ft** width for around 4,023ft runway. Verify the authoritative current width and runway pavement survey before declaring aircraft compatibility. Its second RW10/28 is about **3,200ft**, below 1,200m; current jump/drop-zone procedures require coordination.

**U12 — Stanford Field, Idaho.** FAA information describes a **4,500 × 50ft** paved field with **300ft/540ft displaced thresholds** near 4,966ft elevation. Physical runway length, directional LDA and high-altitude airplane performance must be assessed separately.

**UBE — Cumberland Municipal, Wisconsin.** [Municipal airport operator](https://cityofcumberland.net/airport/) publishes **12,500lb maximum runway weight** and self-service 100LL and Swift UL94, not guaranteed Jet A. Runway structural rating and the airplane's required fuel are distinct issues.

**W63 — Lake Country Regional, Virginia.** A paved runway only around **4,006 × 50ft** requires strict type-specific width and usable length screening. The Regional title does not assign an Airlinerra airport tier or actual air-carrier certificate.

**WLW — Willows/Glenn, California.** [Glenn County](https://www.countyofglenn.net/government/departments/public-works/airports) identifies a **4,125ft** paved primary and separate **3,778ft** secondary; the shorter runway cannot inherit the primary's 1,200m eligibility.

**VCB — Nut Tree, California.** [Solano County](https://www.solanocounty.gov/government/general-services/nut-tree-airport/about) identifies fuel and small/medium business-jet services, which support targeted charter research but are not proof of a licensed public scheduled passenger service.

**W22 — Upshur County, West Virginia.** [Airport authority](https://www.flyw22.com/index.html) confirms air ambulance operations. Emergency medical aviation is a genuine activity, not automatic evidence of an air-cargo route or public scheduled airline.

**1QK, TSP, U70 and CAR:** runway altitude or seasonal conditions and approach-specific usable length require separate fleet screening. **CAR Caribou** must not inherit neighboring **PQI** passenger-airline services.
