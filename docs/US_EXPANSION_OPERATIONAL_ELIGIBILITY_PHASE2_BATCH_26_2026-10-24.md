## Selected source findings and interpretation

### RNT — Renton Municipal, Washington: Boeing factory use is not scheduled airline service

[Renton Airport operator updates](https://www.rentonwa.gov/Government/Departments-and-Offices/Public-Works/Airport) describe general-aviation use alongside Boeing 737 factory test/first flights. The [Taxiway Alpha capital-improvements page](https://www.rentonwa.gov/Government/Departments-and-Offices/Public-Works/Airport/Capital-Improvement-Projects) confirms Phase 1 completed **September 2026**, an October 13 two-hour runway closure was **in the past** at the October 24 review date, and Phase 2 information is expected in **early 2027**. Do not present a past inspection as an ongoing runway shutdown, nor treat airplane factory flights as an operating scheduled passenger airline. Check live FAA/airport notices for any new work.

### S52 — Methow Valley State, Washington: open year-round but with limited winter service

[Washington State DOT](https://wsdot.wa.gov/travel/aviation/airports-list/methow-valley-state) explicitly reports the airport is **open year-round** with **limited winter snow removal**, pilot-operated runway lights with a 15-minute timeout, and airport conditions subject to current NOTAM. The state warns that state-run airfields are principally maintained for emergency use and does not warrant suitability for other purposes. Verify WSDOT owner rights for a regularly scheduled commercial operation, not just paved length.

### SEZ — Sedona, Arizona: voluntary noise guidance, not a permanent curfew

The [Sedona Airport Fly Friendly / noise guidance](https://sedonaairport.org/noise-abatement/) asks pilots to limit operations **between 21:00 and 07:00**, avoid touch-and-go exercises, and follow noise-sensitive flight paths. The airport **expressly says these recommendations are voluntary**; do not claim an absolute enforceable night-time airport closure from them. Mountain terrain, aircraft-specific performance and actual carrier credentials remain unresolved separately.

### S42 — Springer Municipal, New Mexico: runway strength and access, not just length

Retained [FAA-derived Springer listing](https://www.airnav.com/airport/S42) gives a public paved runway about **5,003×60ft**, field elevation **5,894ft**, pavement **8,500lb single-wheel** weight rating, no listed fuel, and ground-access gates that require contacting airport management. This is a potentially consequential aircraft-capacity and access screen even though the runway physically exceeds 1,200m.

### MAN — Nampa Municipal, Idaho: advertised charters plus source code reconciliation

The [City of Nampa airport service directory](https://www.cityofnampa.us/377/Airport-Services) explicitly lists **AvCenter and Owyhee Air under Air Charter**, as well as maintenance and aviation facilities. The airport's candidate registry retains an older `KS67` source identifier while the GPS code is `KMAN`. Confirm FAA historical S67-to-MAN identifier continuity and geolocation under the same immutable airport key, and independently confirm operator certifications and actual aircraft. Advertising charter flights does not establish scheduled airline flights or automatic game acceptance.

### RYY — Cobb County McCollum Field, Georgia: genuine charter business evidence

[Cobb County's airport department](https://www.cobbcounty.gov/transportation/about-department-transportation/airport-division) describes several companies providing charter flights at RYY but no major scheduled airlines. Preserve positive commercial activity rather than classifying charter service as absent. Verify actual operators, certificates, fleets, runway weight and county permissions.

### RSL — Russell Municipal, Kansas: runway source dimensions conflict

The [City of Russell](https://www.russellcity.org/155/Airport) reports a **5,500ft** main runway, whereas earlier FAA-derived runway research and pinned candidate data indicate approximately **5,000ft**. An extra turf crosswind strip is separate. Until the official latest FAA facility record and as-built declared runway distances are reconciled, do not assume either published length for aircraft suitability.

### Additional operational distinctions

- **RPH Graham, Texas** has a published aerobatic practice area; flight windows and multiple-runway compatibility require checks rather than labeling it closed.
- **RUE Russellville, Arkansas** is described by the city as noncommercial with agricultural flights. Confirm whether that means no scheduled airline only or also limits particular charter/cargo operations.
- **RRT Warroad, Minnesota** is near the Canadian border; an 'International' airport name does not automatically grant customs clearance.
- **RVS Tulsa Riverside** is a busy business/general aviation reliever; its FBOs and local flight training don't prove current airline service.
- **S24 Sandusky County** has Sunday FBO service by prior arrangement and medical activity. Staffing constraints are not the same as closure.
- **S33 Madras, Oregon** includes charter business leads; confirm operator license and currently active aviation procedure NOTAMs.
- **SFZ North Central State, Rhode Island** has a qualifying approximately 5,000ft main runway but separate 3,211ft paved secondary. The secondary fails 1,200m; it does not automatically exclude the primary or the airport.
- **SGT Stuttgart, Arkansas** has two runways and agricultural flying, making runway-specific and traffic coordination essential.
